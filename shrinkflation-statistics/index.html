Shrinkflation statistics

By ShrinkRadar · Updated 2026-10-17 · Computed from our live case library on every build

These numbers come from 713 documented shrinkflation cases: packs that got smaller while the price stayed the same or rose, each recorded by a consumer agency, consumer advocate or news report that we link. The typical pack lost 12.5% of its contents, which is a 14.3% hidden price rise per unit. Where the source also recorded the shelf price (412 cases), every one got more expensive per unit, by a median of 20.0%.

Did the price per unit go up?

412 cases have the shelf price before and after, mostly from German and Dutch consumer agencies. All of them got more expensive per unit, with a median real increase of 20.0%. That is partly by design: Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg and Consumentenbond list a smaller pack when it ends up dearer per unit, so read this as how big the hidden rises are, not how often they happen.

How these numbers are counted

One row in our library is one product whose pack got smaller, as recorded by one source (other sources reporting the same change are linked on the case page, not counted again). The cut is (1 − new size ÷ old size). The year is the year the source gives for the change. The real price change uses the shelf prices the source recorded. We do not estimate missing prices.

The library mixes sources with different habits, so compare shares and medians, not raw counts between countries. Figures update automatically when cases are added; see the latest additions. FAQ

How common is shrinkflation?

Very common in packaged groceries. We have documented 713 cases from 14 countries and 423 brands, each with a source, and they are only the cases that a consumer agency, advocate or reporter caught and published. The real number is much higher.

How much do products shrink on average?

The median documented pack got 12.5% smaller (mean 15.8%). That is a 14.3% price rise per gram, millilitre or sheet if the shelf price stayed the same. 259 cases (36%) cut less than 10%, small enough to go unnoticed, and 146 (20%) cut 20% or more.

Which products shrink the most?

By number of cases: chocolate & candy (129), pasta, rice & meals (51), dairy (49), breakfast cereals (46). By size of the cut, the deepest median cuts are in pasta, rice & meals (−20.0%), health & supplements (−20.0%), personal care (−16.7%).

Is shrinkflation getting worse?

Documented cases jumped from 2020 on: 2020: 42, 2021: 49, 2022: 101, 2023: 136, 2024: 146, 2025: 89, 2026: 99. The peak so far is 2024 with 146 cases. 2026 is only counted up to 2026-10-17, so it is not a full year yet. Part of the rise is more reporting, since agencies and newspapers started tracking shrinkflation closely during the 2021–2023 inflation spike.

Which country has the most shrinkflation?

In our library, Germany (335 cases), because Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg publishes every case it confirms. Among English-speaking markets: United States 177, United Kingdom 58, Australia 31, Canada 11, New Zealand 10. That reflects who reports cases, not a measured rate per country.

Does the price go down when the pack shrinks?

Not in our records. Where the source also recorded the shelf price (412 cases), every one got more expensive per unit, by a median of 20.0%. Most of these prices come from consumer agencies that log a pack only when it got dearer per unit, so this shows how big the hidden rises are, not how often a smaller pack comes with a fair price cut.

Can I use these statistics?

Yes. The whole case library is free to download as CSV or JSON under CC BY 4.0 on open data. Please cite "ShrinkRadar" and link this page or the case page.
