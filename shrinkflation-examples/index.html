Shrinkflation examples

By ShrinkRadar · Updated 2026-10-17 · Every example links its source

Shrinkflation is when a pack gets smaller but the price stays the same or goes up. These are 129 real examples, picked from our library of 713 documented cases. They are mostly household names from the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, with one example per brand in each category, and the hidden price rise you pay per unit if the shelf price did not change.

FAQ

What are some examples of shrinkflation?

Well-documented examples include Cadbury Favourites box, 373g (New Zealand) (373 g → 340 g), Charmin Ultra Soft Super Mega Roll (396 sheets/roll → 366 sheets/roll), Dawn Platinum dish soap (32 fl oz → 30 fl oz), Hershey's Dark Chocolate party-size bag (29 oz → 23.9 oz). Each one on this page links to the consumer agency, consumer advocate or news report that recorded the change.

What is the most famous example of shrinkflation?

Toblerone in the UK in 2016 is the best known: the 170 g bar became 150 g by widening the gaps between the peaks, and the 400 g bar became 360 g. Charmin and Cottonelle toilet paper, Doritos bags and Cadbury chocolate are other examples that drew wide press coverage.

Which products shrink the most?

Chocolate and candy, breakfast cereals, coffee, chips and toilet paper come up most often in our library. The median cut among the examples on this page is 11.6%, which is a 13.2% hidden price rise per unit if the price stayed the same.

Is there a full shrinkflation list?

Yes. This page picks well-known examples. The full list of 713 documented cases is on known cases, by year in the shrinkflation lists from 2020 to 2026, and as a free CSV/JSON download on open data.

How do I know these examples are real?

Every example comes from a source we fetched and checked: consumer agencies such as Which?, CHOICE, Consumer NZ and Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg, the Mouse Print* archive, and news investigations. Each case page quotes the source and links it. If we cannot verify a reported shrink, we leave it out.
